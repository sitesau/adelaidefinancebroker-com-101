Build the comparison on four filters

ASIC Moneysmart frames the consumer side of home lending as a careful comparison of loan options, including costs and repayments, not just a headline rate. Carry that discipline into the broker discussion with four filters.

  • Category fit: ask what type of borrowing the broker arranges most often, and whether your enquiry is home, investment, car, business, commercial or equipment finance.
  • Lender realism: ask which lenders are realistic for your situation now, not in theory.
  • Cost drivers: compare fees, features, repayments and any switching costs before accepting a shortlist.
  • Evidence: ask what information would materially improve the shortlist before application, and what remains an estimate until lender review.

What changes by loan category

Decision points move with the purpose of the loan. A home borrower may be weighing repayments, fees and features. An investor may need the file presented to lenders with clear assumptions. A business, commercial or equipment enquiry may turn first on business records, asset purpose or property security. Car finance should still be matched to purpose and records rather than rate headlines alone.

Use Moneysmart as the control test

Before committing, use ASIC Moneysmart as a neutral check on the reasoning. For home loans, compare options and understand the full cost. If refinancing is raised, Moneysmart covers switching home loans and whether a change could save money once costs are considered. If an interest only option is discussed, use the guidance and calculator to understand repayments before and after the interest only period and the added total cost. For investment borrowing, keep the discussion on evidence, documents and structure rather than broad promises. A strong broker answer should narrow the field, explain assumptions, and leave final approval with lender review.

  1. Name the loan type. Decide whether the enquiry is home, investment, car, business, commercial or equipment finance before contacting anyone.
  2. Request the right specialist. Ask to speak with the broker category that matches the borrowing purpose, not a general callback.
  3. Compare cost drivers. Check proposed options against fees, features, repayments and switching costs rather than rate alone.
  4. Check the reasoning. Use ASIC Moneysmart guidance to test whether the recommended structure suits your budget and purpose.

Common questions

Does access to 40+ lenders guarantee the best deal? No. The Adelaide material presents lender access as useful breadth, not proof of fit. A larger panel only matters when the broker can narrow it by loan category, lender realism, fees, features, repayments and any switching costs. The page also says brokers are paid by lenders, so the comparison should still be tested against Moneysmart guidance.

What should I prepare before sending an enquiry? For home or investment borrowing, gather income details, current debts, the broad property plan and questions about structure or repayments. For business, commercial or equipment finance, add recent business information and a clear explanation of what the funds are for. That gives the broker something specific to assess from the first call.

Can an enquiry website provide credit advice? The Adelaide page says it is an enquiry website, that licensed finance brokers provide all credit assistance, and that the site does not provide financial advice or credit assistance. Treat recommendations as the start of diligence: ask what is confirmed, what is assumed, and what still depends on lender review.
